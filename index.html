Virtual Assessment Option

What if the prospective resident lives out of state?

Screen 9 of 12

If face-to-face interaction is not practical, arrange a virtual interaction with the resident and family as part of the preadmission review.

  • General appearance and grooming
  • Alertness and engagement
  • Ability to follow one- or two-step directions
  • Ability to sit, stand, transfer, or walk when safe
  • Communication ability
  • Calmness, irritability, anxiety, or confusion
  • Family interaction
  • Amount of assistance and cueing
Do not let distance become an excuse for making an admission decision with inadequate information.

Important: Virtual interaction is an additional source of information. It does not replace required clinical information or the RAT.

💻

Use technology creatively and responsibly to gather information when distance makes an in-person visit difficult.

Module 6 Regulatory Guide — Admission Decision

Preadmission

  • Obtain and review required assessment information.
  • Evaluate medical, functional, cognitive, behavioral, psychosocial, and nutritional status.
  • Determine whether resident needs can be safely met within the licensed level of care.
  • Assess and document whether awake overnight staff are needed when indicated.
  • Use current health information and applicable assessment timeframes.
Manager test: What do I still need to know about Mrs. Carter before I can responsibly commit to admission?

Admission

  • Base the decision on objective assessment findings.
  • Confirm the program’s demonstrated ability to provide safe, appropriate care.
  • Do not accept needs that cannot be safely managed simply because a room is available.
  • Explain honestly what the program can and cannot provide.
  • Document the rationale for the decision.
Manager test: If I had to explain this admission to a surveyor six months from now, could I show why it was reasonable?

Through the Surveyor’s Eyes

Survey review commonly focuses on whether required assessments were completed, resident needs were identified accurately, and the program admitted only residents whose needs could safely be met.

Think like the surveyor: What in the record proves that the decision was based on current resident needs rather than census pressure, family urgency, or convenience?
